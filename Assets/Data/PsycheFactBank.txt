The asteroid Psyche is one of the largest objects in the main asteroid belt, measuring about 226 kilometers (140 miles) across at its widest point.
Unlike most asteroids made mostly of rock and ice, Psyche contains a large amount of metal, including iron and nickel.
Scientists believe Psyche may be the exposed metal core of an early planet that never fully formed.
Studying Psyche could give scientists a rare look at what planetary cores are like beneath the surfaces of rocky planets such as Earth.
The Psyche mission is the first NASA mission dedicated to studying a metal-rich asteroid.
The Psyche spacecraft launched on October 13, 2023 from Kennedy Space Center in Florida.
The spacecraft is expected to arrive at the asteroid Psyche in 2029 after traveling billions of kilometers through space.
Psyche is located in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, about three times farther from the Sun than Earth.
The Psyche spacecraft uses solar-electric propulsion, which is powered by sunlight and is more efficient than traditional rocket engines for long-distance travel.
When the spacecraft reaches Psyche, it will orbit the asteroid for about two years to collect scientific data.
The mission carries instruments that will study Psyche’s composition, magnetic field, and surface features.
Scientists will measure Psyche’s gravity and density to better understand what it is made of inside.
Psyche is also known as “16 Psyche” because it was the 16th asteroid ever discovered.
Because Psyche may be a planetary core, studying it could help scientists understand how planets formed early in the solar system.
Much about Psyche remains a mystery, and one of the mission’s main goals is to answer questions scientists have never been able to study up close before.
